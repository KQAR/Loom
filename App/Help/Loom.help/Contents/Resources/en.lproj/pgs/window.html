Loom Help

Reading a capture

The main window is the working surface: a sidebar that narrows what you are looking at, a table of exchanges, and an inspector underneath. Open it from the Loom wordmark at the bottom of the console, or from the Dock.

The sidebar

Top section: the whole capture sliced by kind — All Flows, Requests, Connections (relayed tunnels, which have no body to read), Errors. Below those are panels rather than filters: Rules, Audit, Breakpoints.

Devices is a tree. Each device Loom has seen — this Mac, a phone, another machine — holds the apps whose traffic it could attribute, so you can ask for one app on one device. Local apps are identified exactly, by process; a phone's apps are named from what they say about themselves in User-Agent, which is a claim rather than a measurement.

Hosts folds by domain, and the hosts Loom is decrypting sort first, since those are the ones with something to read.

Selecting more than one row

Selection is a set, and it combines the way you would want rather than the way that is easiest to implement:

  • Two hosts means either host.
  • A host plus a device means that host's traffic from that device.

In other words: rows in the same group widen the list, rows in different groups narrow it. If a selection ends up admitting nothing, the empty state says which rows you picked and offers a way back.

The table

One row per exchange, newest at the bottom, following the tail until you scroll away. The columns cover what you normally sort or scan by — status, app, whether it was decrypted, protocol, method, host, path, and time.

Under the table, the chip strip is a quick filter: protocol, content type, status class. Chips in one group widen, chips in different groups narrow — same rule as the sidebar.

Press ⌘F for the find bar. It can search the URL within this window, or ask the engine to search stored headers and bodies, which reaches further back than the rows currently loaded.

The inspector

Select a row and the pane below fills in: request and response headers in wire order (duplicates and all), the body with JSON, XML, HTML and image rendering, the timing breakdown, and the connection's own facts — which TLS versions each leg negotiated, whether the upstream socket was reused, how many bytes the compressed body actually was.

Timing answers "why is this slow" in parts rather than as one number: DNS, TCP and the TLS handshake are counted separately and charged to the exchange that opened the connection, so a request that reused a socket honestly shows none of them. What is left is the server's think time and the body transfer.

Housekeeping

The toolbar's record button pauses capture without stopping the proxy — traffic keeps flowing, Loom just stops writing it down. The eraser clears what has been captured. Both are also reachable by an agent, so if the dot and the button disagree with what you remember doing, something else changed it; the window re-reads the engine rather than trusting its own copy.
